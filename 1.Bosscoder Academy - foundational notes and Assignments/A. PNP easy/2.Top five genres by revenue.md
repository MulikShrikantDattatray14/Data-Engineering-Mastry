# 2. Top five genres by revenue

## Question and image

Find the five genres that generated the most revenue in the Chinook store. Line-level revenue is `InvoiceLine.UnitPrice * InvoiceLine.Quantity`. Join `InvoiceLine` → `Track` → `Genre` to attribute each sold line to a genre.

Return exactly two columns:

- `Genre` — the genre name (`Genre.Name`).
- `Revenue` — the total revenue for that genre, rounded to **2 decimal places**.

Sort by `Revenue` in **descending** order; break ties by `Genre` ascending. Return only the **top 5 rows**.

**Expected output:** `Genre`, `Revenue` · 5 rows · order matters.

## My solution — SQLite

```sql
select Genre.Name as Genre , round (sum(InvoiceLine.UnitPrice * InvoiceLine.Quantity),2) as Revenue
from InvoiceLine
join Track on InvoiceLine.TrackId = Track.TrackId
join  Genre on Genre.GenreId=Track.GenreId
group by Genre.GenreId
order by Revenue desc, Genre asc
limit 5
```

**1. Calculate the revenue of each sold line**

A line can sell more than one unit, so use InvoiceLine.UnitPrice * InvoiceLine.Quantity, not just the price.

```sql
SELECT InvoiceLineId, TrackId, UnitPrice, Quantity,
       UnitPrice * Quantity AS LineRevenue
FROM InvoiceLine;
```

Result:

| InvoiceLineId | TrackId | UnitPrice | Quantity | LineRevenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 101 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | 102 | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 3 | 201 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| 4 | 301 | 1.5 | 2 | 3 |
| 5 | 401 | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 6 | 501 | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| 7 | 601 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 8 | 101 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

For example, line 2 sells three units at 1 each, contributing 3. Track 101 is sold on two lines; both sales must contribute.

**2. Connect each sale to its genre**

InvoiceLine identifies the sold track. Track supplies GenreId, and Genre supplies the readable name.

```sql
SELECT InvoiceLine.InvoiceLineId, Track.Name AS TrackName, Genre.Name AS Genre,
       InvoiceLine.UnitPrice * InvoiceLine.Quantity AS LineRevenue
FROM InvoiceLine
JOIN Track ON InvoiceLine.TrackId = Track.TrackId
JOIN Genre ON Genre.GenreId = Track.GenreId
ORDER BY InvoiceLine.InvoiceLineId;
```

Result:

| InvoiceLineId | TrackName | Genre | LineRevenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Stone Road | Rock | 2 |
| 2 | Night Train | Rock | 3 |
| 3 | Blue Sky | Jazz | 4 |
| 4 | Morning Suite | Classical | 3 |
| 5 | Sunshine | Pop | 3 |
| 6 | Slow River | Blues | 2 |
| 7 | Iron Gate | Metal | 1 |
| 8 | Stone Road | Rock | 1 |

All eight sold lines remain, each with one genre. The joins identify the genre without losing repeated purchases.

**3. Add line revenue within each genre**

Group by GenreId as in the saved solution, then sum revenue and round the total to two decimal places.

```sql
SELECT Genre.Name AS Genre,
       ROUND(SUM(InvoiceLine.UnitPrice * InvoiceLine.Quantity), 2) AS Revenue
FROM InvoiceLine
JOIN Track ON InvoiceLine.TrackId = Track.TrackId
JOIN Genre ON Genre.GenreId = Track.GenreId
GROUP BY Genre.GenreId;
```

Result:

| Genre | Revenue |
| --- | --- |
| Rock | 6 |
| Jazz | 4 |
| Classical | 3 |
| Pop | 3 |
| Blues | 2 |
| Metal | 1 |

Rock totals 2 + 3 + 1 = 6. Jazz totals 4. Classical and Pop both total 3, Blues totals 2, and Metal totals 1. There are six groups before limiting.

**4. Assemble the final query**

Combine the steps using the saved solution. The query below preserves the original logic.

```sql
select Genre.Name as Genre , round (sum(InvoiceLine.UnitPrice * InvoiceLine.Quantity),2) as Revenue
from InvoiceLine
join Track on InvoiceLine.TrackId = Track.TrackId
join  Genre on Genre.GenreId=Track.GenreId
group by Genre.GenreId
order by Revenue desc, Genre asc
limit 5
```

Final result for this example:

| Genre | Revenue |
| --- | --- |
| Rock | 6 |
| Jazz | 4 |
| Classical | 3 |
| Pop | 3 |
| Blues | 2 |

**Check the result:** ORDER BY Revenue DESC puts the most revenue first; Genre ASC puts Classical before Pop when both have 3. LIMIT 5 excludes Metal, the sixth genre. The saved SQLite grouping uses GenreId; each ID has one associated genre name.

## My solution — SQL Server

```sql
select TOP (5) Genre.Name as Genre , round (sum(InvoiceLine.UnitPrice * InvoiceLine.Quantity),2) as Revenue
from InvoiceLine
join Track on InvoiceLine.TrackId = Track.TrackId
join  Genre on Genre.GenreId=Track.GenreId
group by Genre.GenreId, Genre.Name
order by Revenue desc, Genre asc
```

## Official solution

```sql
SELECT g.Name AS Genre,
       ROUND(SUM(il.UnitPrice * il.Quantity), 2) AS Revenue
FROM InvoiceLine il
JOIN Track t ON il.TrackId = t.TrackId
JOIN Genre g ON t.GenreId = g.GenreId
GROUP BY g.Name
ORDER BY Revenue DESC, Genre ASC
LIMIT 5;
```

### What this tests

Joining multiple tables, calculating revenue, grouping, rounding, and returning the top results.

### The approach

Join invoice lines to tracks and genres; sum line price × quantity by genre; round to two decimal places; sort by revenue descending and genre ascending; keep five rows.

### Why this way

The direct joins connect each sale to its genre, allowing one grouped revenue calculation.

### Common mistakes

Selecting track names instead of genre names, omitting LIMIT 5, or using joins that duplicate sales and inflate revenue.
