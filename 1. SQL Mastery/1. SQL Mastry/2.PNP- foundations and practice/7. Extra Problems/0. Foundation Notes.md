# Extra Problems - Quick Revision Notes

**Main idea:** this section contains the five product-rating extensions supplied in the PrepNPlaced ZIP. They have no answer key. Use the relevant Joins, Aggregation, and Windowing notes as needed; first decide whether your result is one row per product, category, brand, or rating band.

The notes below explain reusable ideas. They do not solve the five practice problems.

## 1. Know the useful columns and grain

| Table | Useful fields | One row represents |
|---|---|---|
| Products | ProductID, CategoryID, Sub_Category, Brand, Rating | One catalogue product |
| Category | CategoryID, CategoryName | One named category |
| OrderDetails | OrderDetailID, ProductID, Quantity | One ordered product line |

`Products.Rating` is out of 5 and can be `NULL` when a product is unrated. A product can have many OrderDetails rows or none. Joining a product to several lines repeats its rating, so check the grain before averaging ratings.

## 2. Distinguish missing from zero

| Value or case | Meaning to check |
|---|---|
| `Rating IS NULL` | No recorded rating |
| `Quantity = 0` | A recorded line with zero units, if permitted by the data |
| No matching OrderDetails row | Product has no recorded order line |
| CategoryID has no Category match | Product exists, but its category label is missing |

These cases need not mean the same thing. If the report must include products without sales or categories without a matching dimension row, choose the starting table and join direction accordingly.

## 3. Calculate at the right level before comparing

For a product with two order lines of 2 and 3 units, its total units are `2 + 3 = 5`. The product's rating still counts once in a product-level average. Build one row per product before comparing ratings with product sales or taking an average across products.

| Stage | Example grain |
|---|---|
| Raw OrderDetails | One row per order line |
| Units summary | One row per ProductID |
| Product-level comparison | One row per product, including a rating and units |

A median is the middle value after sorting. For `1, 3, 5` it is 3; for `1, 3, 5, 7` it is the average of 3 and 5, which is 4. Establish whether products with no lines contribute zero units before finding the median.

## 4. Define numerator, denominator, and boundaries

For a share, write down what is counted in the numerator and denominator before dividing. `COUNT(column)` skips NULL values; `COUNT(*)` counts rows. When creating rating bands, make every boundary unambiguous: for example, `Rating < 3` and `Rating >= 3 AND Rating < 4` do not overlap. Give unrated products their own band only when requested.

Do not average a per-order or per-line number when the question asks for an average **per product**. First calculate each product's measure, then average those product rows.

## Quick check

1. What does one result row represent?
2. Do products without ratings, without order lines, or without a matching category stay in scope?
3. Did a join duplicate products before a count or average?
4. Are rating-band boundaries complete and non-overlapping?
5. If the source offers no answer key, can you verify your query on a small hand-calculated example?
