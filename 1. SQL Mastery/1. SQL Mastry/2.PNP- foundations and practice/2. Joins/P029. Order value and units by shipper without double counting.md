# PNP Q029. Order value and units by shipper without double counting

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![MEDIUM](<../../_badges/medium.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 29, pp. 49–50. **Difficulty:** Medium.

**Table colors** · 🟩 `Shippers` · 🟦 `Orders` · 🟥 `OrderDetails`

**Business scenario.** The logistics finance team is renegotiating courier rate cards. For each shipper they want three numbers side by side: how many orders it carried, the total order value it carried, and how many units were inside those orders. Last quarter an analyst produced this report and the order value came out higher than the finance ledger. Your job is to produce it correctly.

**Task.** For every shipper that carried at least one order, return the number of orders, the sum of 🟦 `Total_Order_Amount` for those orders, and the total units (🟥 `Quantity`) in those orders. Orders with a missing or unrecognised `ShipperID` should appear under one row labelled `Unknown shipper`. Sort by order value, highest first.

### Required output columns

| Column | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `shipper_name` | The shipper’s company name, or `Unknown shipper`. | 🟩 `Shippers` |
| `orders` | Number of orders carried. | 🟦 `Orders` |
| `order_value` | Total of those orders’ amounts, with each order counted once. | 🟦 `Orders` |
| `units_shipped` | Sum of the quantities on those orders’ detail lines. | 🟥 `OrderDetails` |

### How the sample produces this output

🟩 **FastShip** carried orders 10 and 11, worth 🟦 `100 + 200 = 300`. Order 10 contains 🟥 `2 + 3 = 5` units and order 11 contains another 4, giving 🟥 `5 + 4 = 9` units across two orders.

🟩 **SlowShip** carried order 12, worth 🟦 **50**, with 🟥 **1 unit**. FastShip appears first because its order value is higher. Every sample order has a recognized shipper ID.

<details>
<summary>Agreed Step 1 · Why this table order and these joins?</summary>

**First prepare 🟥 `OrderDetails_2`.** The plan is one row per `OrderID` that has detail lines. Order 10’s quantities 2 and 3 become one total of 5 units, so its amount of 100 appears once when the total is attached.

| Join step | Why the table belongs here | What a missing match does |
|---|---|---|
| First · 🟦 `FROM Orders` | One row represents one order. This is where its amount and both matching IDs are available. | Every order enters the report, including one with a missing shipper or no detail rows. |
| Second · 🟩 `LEFT JOIN Shippers` on `Orders.ShipperID = Shippers.ShipperID` | Add the company name to each order while keeping every order on the left. | Order 15 survives an unrecognised shipper ID. Its joined name is `NULL` and its final reporting label is `Unknown shipper`. |
| Third · 🟥 `LEFT JOIN OrderDetails_2` on `Orders.OrderID = OrderDetails_2.OrderID` | The previous result still carries the order ID. Attach at most one unit total to each order and keep orders without details. | Order 16 survives with its amount of 80 and `NULL` units immediately after the join. |

The two right-hand sources each match directly to 🟦 `Orders`. Their positions can be exchanged with these keys and joins. Grouping the 🟥 details beforehand gives each order at most one matching units total.
