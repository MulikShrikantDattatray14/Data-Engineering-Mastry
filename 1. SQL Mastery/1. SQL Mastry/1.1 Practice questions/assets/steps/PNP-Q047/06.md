# Q047 · Why use HAVING for the three-product rule?

[![Previous](../previous.svg)](05.md) [![Next](../next.svg)](07.md) · **6 of 8**

The minimum product count is evaluated per brand after the groups and their aggregates have been formed. That makes it a HAVING condition, not a row-level WHERE condition.

## SQL added or changed

```sql
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT P.ProductID) >= 3
```

## Input to this step

| 🟪 Brand | 🟩 products_listed | 🟦 products_sold | 🟦 units_sold | 🟪 list_price_value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Nova | 3 | 2 | 4 | 50 |
| Unbranded | 3 | 2 | 3 | 16 |
| Solo | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

## Result · 🟪 2 qualifying groups

| 🟪 Brand | 🟩 products_listed | 🟦 products_sold | 🟦 units_sold | 🟪 list_price_value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Nova | 3 | 2 | 4 | 50 |
| Unbranded | 3 | 2 | 3 | 16 |

Solo is excluded because it has only two catalogue products. Its zero sales do not cause the exclusion; the product-count threshold does.

<details>
<summary>Full SQLite query for this step</summary>

```sql
SELECT COALESCE(P.Brand, 'Unbranded') AS Brand,
       COUNT(DISTINCT P.ProductID) AS products_listed,
       COUNT(DISTINCT OD.ProductID) AS products_sold,
       COALESCE(SUM(OD.Quantity), 0) AS units_sold,
       COALESCE(SUM(P.Sale_Price * OD.Quantity), 0) AS list_price_value
FROM Products AS P
LEFT JOIN OrderDetails AS OD
    ON P.ProductID = OD.ProductID
GROUP BY COALESCE(P.Brand, 'Unbranded')
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT P.ProductID) >= 3;
```

</details>
