# PNP Q040. Days from sign-up to first order

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![MEDIUM](<../../_badges/medium.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 40, pp. 68–70. **Difficulty:** Medium.

**Primary topic:** MIN per group with a date difference. **Also covers:** CTE then JOIN, Negative-gap data flag.

**Business scenario.** The onboarding team is testing a welcome-coupon email and needs to know when to send it. If most customers buy within a week of signing up, the email should go out on day one; if they take a month, it can wait. For every customer who has ordered, they want the number of days between sign-up and the first order. Rows where the first order seems to come before sign-up must be flagged, not hidden, because they point to a data problem.

**Task.** For each customer with at least one order, return their sign-up date, the date of their first order, the number of days between them, and a data_flag of Order before sign-up when that number is negative (NULL otherwise). Sort by days to first order, flagged rows first.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
CustomerID
DateEntered : sign-up date
first_order_date : earliest OrderDate for the customer
days_to_first_order : first_order_date - DateEntered in whole days
data_flag : Order before sign-up when negative, otherwise NULL
```

**Tables required:** Customers , Orders.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** MIN as "first per group", aggregating before joining, date subtraction, CASE as a data-quality flag.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.
