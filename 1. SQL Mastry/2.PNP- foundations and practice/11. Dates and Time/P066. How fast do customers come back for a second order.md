# PNP Q066. How fast do customers come back for a second order?

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 66, pp. 118–121. **Difficulty:** Hard.

**Primary topic:** Repeat purchase behaviour. **Also covers:** LEAD and ROW_NUMBER, PERCENTILE_CONT, Multiple CTEs.

**Business scenario.** The retention team is deciding when to send the "come back" reminder after a customer's first purchase. Send it too early and it annoys people who were going to return anyway; too late and the customer has gone elsewhere. They want, for each year of first purchase, how many customers ever placed a second order and how long the typical customer took to do it, using the median and the 75th percentile because a few very slow returners would drag a mean.

**Task.** Group customers by the calendar year of their first order. For each year, return the number of customers, how many placed a second order on a later day, the percentage that did, and the median and 75th-percentile number of days from the first order to the second. A second order placed on the same day as the first is treated as part of the first visit.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
first_order_year : year of the customer's first order
customers : customers whose first order fell in that year
customers_with_second_order : those with an order on a later day
pct_repeating : percentage of customers , 2 dp
median_days_to_second : median days from first to second order, among those who came back
p75_days_to_second : 75th percentile of the same
```

**Tables required:** Orders.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** ROW_NUMBER to find the first order, LEAD to fetch the next one, COUNT(column) skipping NULLs, PERCENTILE_CONT ... WITHIN GROUP , same-day deduplication.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.
