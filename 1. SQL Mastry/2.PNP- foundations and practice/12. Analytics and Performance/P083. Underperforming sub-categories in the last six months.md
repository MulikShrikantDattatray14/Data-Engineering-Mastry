# PNP Q083. Underperforming sub-categories in the last six months

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![HARD](<../../_badges/hard.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 83, pp. 158–160. **Difficulty:** Hard.

**Primary topic:** PERCENTILE_CONT per group as a relative threshold. **Also covers:** Anchored date window, LEFT JOIN to keep zero sellers, Normalising by group size, Multiple CTEs, Flag normalisation.

**Business scenario.** Category managers review their ranges twice a year and decide where to cut listings or push promotions. A fixed cut-off like "fewer than 500 units" does not work: 500 units is a lot for water and nothing for skin care. Raw units are also unfair to small sub- categories, which simply list fewer products. So each category gets its own bar, measured per listed product: a sub-category underperforms if its units sold per listed product in the last six months fall below the 25th percentile of that figure across the sub-categories of the same category. Only active categories are reviewed.

**Task.** For every Sub_Category in an active category, compute units sold in the six months ending on the latest OrderDate in the data (0 if none), the number of products listed, and units per listed product. Compute each category's 25th percentile of units per listed product. Return sub-categories strictly below their category's threshold. Sort by category, then units per product, lowest first.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
CategoryName , Sub_Category
listed_products : products in the sub-category
units_last_6m : SUM(Quantity) in the window, 0 when nothing sold
units_per_product : units_last_6m / listed_products , 2 decimals
category_p25 : the category's 25th percentile of units_per_product , 2 decimals
```

**Tables required:** Orders , OrderDetails , Products , Category.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** anchoring a rolling window to the data, aggregating lines to the product grain and then to the sub-category, keeping zero sellers with a LEFT JOIN, PERCENTILE_CONT as a per-group threshold, joining a threshold back to the rows it judges.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.

### Sample input and expected output

*Illustrative example; assume a continuous 25th percentile. Four sub-categories each list one product, with units per product of 0, 10, 20, and 30; the percentile is 7.50.*

Input — `Category`:

| CategoryID | CategoryName | Active |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Care | Y |

Input — `Products`:

| ProductID | CategoryID | Sub_Category |
|---:|---:|---|
| 101 | 1 | A |
| 102 | 1 | B |
| 103 | 1 | C |
| 104 | 1 | D |

Input — `Orders` and `OrderDetails` (all dates are in the six months ending 2026-06-30):

| OrderID | OrderDate | ProductID | Quantity |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2026-06-30 | 102 | 10 |
| 2 | 2026-06-30 | 103 | 20 |
| 3 | 2026-06-30 | 104 | 30 |

Expected output:

| CategoryName | Sub_Category | listed_products | units_last_6m | units_per_product | category_p25 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Care | A | 1 | 0 | 0.00 | 7.50 |
