# PNP Q032. Products priced above their category average

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![MEDIUM](<../../_badges/medium.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 32, pp. 54–56. **Difficulty:** Medium.

**Primary topic:** Correlated subquery. **Also covers:** Window AVG with PARTITION BY as an alternative.

**Business scenario.** The pricing team is reviewing products that sit at the expensive end of their own category before a festive sale. A product at 600 is cheap in cookware and expensive in snacks, so a single catalogue-wide cut-off is useless. The festive sale is in Beverages first, so they start there, and they want each product compared only with the products in its own category.

**Task.** For the Beverages category ( CategoryID 5009), return every product whose Sale_Price is strictly greater than the average Sale_Price of all products with the same CategoryID . Show the category average next to it. Sort by CategoryID , then price, highest first.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
ProductID , Product , CategoryID , Sale_Price
category_avg_price : average Sale_Price of products in the same CategoryID (2 dp)
```

**Tables required:** Products.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** correlated subquery in WHERE and SELECT, outer-row references, NULL behaviour in the correlation, window AVG as a rewrite.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.

### Interview follow-up (Step 9)

The team now wants products more than 25% above their category average. Which single expression changes, and how would you show the percentage gap as a column?

**Expected output**

| ProductID | Product | CategoryID | Sale_Price | category_avg_price |

| 3 | Juice | 5009 | 300 | 200.00 |

Only Beverages products contribute to the 200 category average.
