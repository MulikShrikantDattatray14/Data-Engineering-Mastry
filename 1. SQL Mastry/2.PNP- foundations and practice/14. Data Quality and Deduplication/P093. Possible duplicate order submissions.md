# PNP Q093. Possible duplicate order submissions

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![EXPERT](<../../_badges/expert.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 93, pp. 186–189. **Difficulty:** Expert.

**Primary topic:** Duplicate detection. **Also covers:** Line signature with ordered STRING_AGG, Self join on orders, NOT EXISTS to pick the earliest original, Two-level aggregation, IS NOT DISTINCT FROM.

**Business scenario.** Customer support keeps getting complaints from people charged twice: they tapped "Place order" again when the app hung, and two orders went through. The risk team wants a daily control that says whether any double submissions happened, plus the list support needs to cancel the copies before they ship. The data platform team will use the same list to keep duplicates out of order counts and revenue.

**Task.** Build the nightly control for double submissions. Pair every two orders placed by the same customer on the same OrderDate , and report in one row: how many such pairs exist, how many have exactly the same basket (same products in the same quantities), how many have the same amount, how many are exact copies (same basket and same header: amount, payment method, shipper), and a verdict. The row-level list of suspected duplicates, each pointing at its original, goes in Step 10.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
same_day_pairs : pairs of orders from one customer on one day
same_basket_pairs : of those, pairs with identical baskets
same_amount_pairs : pairs with identical Total_Order_Amount
exact_copies : same basket and same header
verdict : No duplicate submissions or Review: send same-basket pairs to support
```

**Tables required:** Orders , OrderDetails.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** collapsing many rows into one comparable value with ordered STRING_AGG, a self join with an inequality to avoid mirrored pairs, NOT EXISTS to anchor every copy on the first order, NULL-safe row comparison.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.

### Interview follow-up (Step 9)

If Orders had an order timestamp, how would you change the match so that two identical orders 40 seconds apart are flagged but the same basket bought again at 9 pm is not? What threshold would you choose, and how would you check it?
