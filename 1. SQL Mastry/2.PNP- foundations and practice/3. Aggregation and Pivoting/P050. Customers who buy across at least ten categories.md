# PNP Q050. Customers who buy across at least ten categories

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![MEDIUM](<../../_badges/medium.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 50, pp. 86–88. **Difficulty:** Medium.

**Primary topic:** COUNT DISTINCT with HAVING over joins. **Also covers:** Ordered list aggregation (STRING_AGG / ARRAY_AGG), Multi-table join.

**Business scenario.** The CRM team is building a "whole basket" segment for a loyalty pilot. A customer who already buys from several parts of the store treats the shop as their main grocer and is worth a premium tier. A "broad buyer" is a customer whose order lines, across all time, cover at least ten different product categories. The campaign team wants the category IDs in one readable column.

**Task.** For every customer whose order lines cover ten or more distinct CategoryID values, return the number of distinct categories, the category IDs as a comma-separated list in ascending numeric order, and the number of distinct orders that contain lines. Sort by categories bought (most first), then orders (most first), then customer ID.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
CustomerID
categories_bought : distinct CategoryID values across the customer's lines
category_ids : those IDs as text, for example 1, 2, 5 , in numeric order
orders : distinct orders with at least one line
```

**Tables required:** Orders , OrderDetails , Products.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** joining across three tables to reach the attribute you need, COUNT DISTINCT after a fan-out, HAVING on a distinct count, aggregating values into an ordered list.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.
