# PNP Q052. Where shipping time goes: dispatch vs transit by shipper

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 52, pp. 90–91. **Difficulty:** Medium.

**Primary topic:** Averages with validity filters (FILTER). **Also covers:** Date differences, LEFT JOIN with label, CTE.

**Business scenario.** Customers complain that deliveries are slow, and the warehouse and the couriers blame each other. The logistics head wants each shipper's delivery time split into two parts: dispatch (order placed to handed to the courier, which the warehouse controls) and transit (handed over to delivered, which the courier controls). The two parts must add up to the total, or the meeting will argue about the arithmetic instead of the fix.

**Task.** For each shipper, using only orders that have both a ship date and a delivery date in a valid sequence (order â‰¤ ship â‰¤ delivery), return the number of orders measured, average days to ship, average days in transit and average total days. Also show how many of the shipper's orders were excluded. Orders with a missing or unknown ShipperID go under Unknown shipper . Sort by average total days, slowest first.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
shipper_name : CompanyName , or Unknown shipper
orders_measured : orders that pass the validity rule
avg_days_to_ship : average of ship date minus order date, 2 decimals
avg_days_in_transit : average of delivery date minus ship date, 2 decimals
avg_days_total : average of delivery date minus order date, 2 decimals
orders_excluded : the shipper's orders that fail the rule
```

**Tables required:** Orders , Shippers.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** date subtraction, aggregate FILTER clauses, keeping every average on the same population, labelling unmatched keys.
