# PNP Q077. Single-source products and the value at risk

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![HARD](<../../_badges/hard.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 77, pp. 144–146. **Difficulty:** Hard.

**Primary topic:** COUNT(DISTINCT) with a single-value filter. **Also covers:** Window SUM for share of total, Multiple CTEs, LEFT JOIN for orphan keys, List-price value.

**Business scenario.** The supply chain risk team wants to know which products depend on one supplier only. If that supplier has a strike, a flood or a customs hold, the product goes out of stock. For each such product they need the supplier, its country (cross-border suppliers carry extra risk), and how much of the business sits on that product, so they can decide where to qualify a second supplier first.

**Task.** Find every product whose order lines, across all history, name exactly one supplier and never leave the supplier blank. For each, show the supplier, the supplier's country, the product's list-price value, and that value as a percentage of the list-price value of all products. Sort by value, highest first.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
ProductID
Product : product name, or Unknown product when the line's ProductID has no catalogue row
sole_supplier : CompanyName , or Unknown supplier when the SupplierID has no supplier row
supplier_country : Country , or Unknown
list_price_value : SUM(Quantity * Sale_Price) for the product
pct_of_total_value : share of list-price value across all products, single-source or not
```

**Tables required:** OrderDetails , Products , Suppliers.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** COUNT(DISTINCT) at the product grain, NULL behaviour in COUNT(DISTINCT) , window SUM() OVER () for a grand total, filter after the window, LEFT JOIN for orphan keys, list-price value.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.

### Interview follow-up (Step 9)

The team wants to see each supplier's total single-source exposure, so they can tell which supplier's failure would hurt most. How do you roll this result up, and does the share still add correctly?

### Sample input and expected output

*Illustrative example; only product 101 has order lines from exactly one nonblank supplier. The denominator includes every product's line value.*

Input — `Products`:

| ProductID | Product | Sale_Price |
|---:|---|---:|
| 101 | Keyboard | 10 |
| 102 | Mouse | 10 |
| 103 | Cable | 10 |

Input — `Suppliers`:

| SupplierID | CompanyName | Country |
|---:|---|---|
| 7 | Alpha | India |
| 8 | Beta | India |
| 9 | Gamma | Nepal |

Input — `OrderDetails`:

| OrderDetailID | ProductID | Quantity | SupplierID |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 101 | 2 | 7 |
| 2 | 102 | 3 | 8 |
| 3 | 102 | 1 | 9 |
| 4 | 103 | 1 | NULL |

Expected output (product 101 value 20 ÷ total value 70 = 28.57%):

| ProductID | Product | sole_supplier | supplier_country | list_price_value | pct_of_total_value |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| 101 | Keyboard | Alpha | India | 20 | 28.57 |
