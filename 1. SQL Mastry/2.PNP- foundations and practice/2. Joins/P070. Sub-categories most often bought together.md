# PNP Q070. Sub-categories most often bought together

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 70, pp. 128–129. **Difficulty:** Hard.

**Primary topic:** Self join for pair counting. **Also covers:** DISTINCT before pairing, Inequality join to drop mirrored pairs, RANK for top N with ties, Choosing a grain dense enough to count.

**Business scenario.** The merchandising team is planning cross-category placements: which aisles to put next to each other in the app and which sub-categories to suggest at checkout. They want the ten pairs of sub-categories that appear in the same order most often. A pair counts once per order, whatever the quantities or the number of products from each side, and (A, B) is the same pair as (B, A). Placement is decided per aisle, so they count pairs of sub-categories rather than individual products; a single aisle holds hundreds of products, and a product-level ranking would split that demand across them.

**Task.** Find every pair of different Sub_Category values that appear together in at least one order, count the distinct orders containing both, and return the top ten pairs by that count. Keep any pairs tied with tenth place.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
sub_category_a : the alphabetically first sub-category of the pair
sub_category_b : the second
orders_together : number of distinct orders containing both
```

**Tables required:** OrderDetails , Products.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** self join on OrderID , b.x > a.x to drop mirrored pairs and self-pairs, deduplicating to the grain of the question before pairing, RANK for top N with ties.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.
