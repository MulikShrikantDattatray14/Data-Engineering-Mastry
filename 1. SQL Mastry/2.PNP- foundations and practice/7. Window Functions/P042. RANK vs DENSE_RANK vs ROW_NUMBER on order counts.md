# PNP Q042. RANK vs DENSE_RANK vs ROW_NUMBER on order counts

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![MEDIUM](<../../_badges/medium.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 42, pp. 72–74. **Difficulty:** Medium.

**Primary topic:** Ranking function comparison. **Also covers:** Ties, Aggregation before ranking.

**Business scenario.** The CRM team runs a loyalty tier scheme based on order count. They have had three different "top 10" lists from three analysts, with different people on each, and suspect the ranking function is the cause. Order counts are small whole numbers, so many customers share the same count and ties are common. They want all three numbering schemes side by side for every customer.

**Task.** For each customer who has placed an order, return the number of orders placed and three rank columns computed over that count, highest first: ROW_NUMBER , RANK and DENSE_RANK . Sort by the row number.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
CustomerID
orders_placed : number of orders
row_num : ROW_NUMBER() , unique per customer, ties broken by CustomerID
rank_num : RANK() , ties share a rank and the next rank skips
dense_rank_num : DENSE_RANK() , ties share a rank and the next rank does not skip
```

**Tables required:** Orders.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** the three ranking functions and how each treats ties, ranking over an aggregate, deterministic tie-breaking.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.
