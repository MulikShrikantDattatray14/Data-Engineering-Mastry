# PNP Q058. Each customer's latest order

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![MEDIUM](<../../_badges/medium.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 58, pp. 100–102. **Difficulty:** Medium.

**Primary topic:** ROW_NUMBER for the latest record per group. **Also covers:** Deterministic tie-break, DISTINCT ON alternative, LEFT JOIN for names.

**Business scenario.** The CRM team is setting up a "how was your last order?" message. For every customer who has ordered, they need exactly one row: the most recent order, its date and its amount. Customers sometimes place two orders on the same day, and the message tool will break if a customer appears twice, so the rule for that case is: the order with the higher OrderID counts as the later one.

**Task.** For every customer in Orders , return their latest order by OrderDate , breaking same-day ties by the higher OrderID . Return exactly one row per customer. Customers missing from Customers are labelled Unknown customer . Sort by latest order date, most recent first, then customer ID.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
CustomerID
customer_name : first and last name, or Unknown customer
latest_order_id
latest_order_date
latest_order_amount : Total_Order_Amount of that order
```

**Tables required:** Orders , Customers.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** ROW_NUMBER with PARTITION BY and ORDER BY, deterministic tie-breaking, filtering a window result, DISTINCT ON in PostgreSQL.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.

### Sample input and expected output

*Illustrative example; the source gives output columns but no sample rows. Only the columns needed for this example are shown.*

**Input — `Customers`**

| CustomerID | FirstName | LastName |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asha | Rao |
| 2 | Ben | Das |

**Input — `Orders`**

| OrderID | CustomerID | OrderDate | Total_Order_Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 2025-01-01 | 100 |
| 2 | 1 | 2025-01-02 | 200 |
| 3 | 1 | 2025-01-02 | 300 |
| 4 | 2 | 2025-01-01 | 50 |

**Expected output**

| CustomerID | customer_name | latest_order_id | latest_order_date | latest_order_amount |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asha Rao | 3 | 2025-01-02 | 300 |
| 2 | Ben Das | 4 | 2025-01-01 | 50 |

Orders 2 and 3 share a date; the higher OrderID (3) wins for Asha.
