# PNP Q097. What do customers buy on their second order?

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![UNRATED](<../../_badges/unrated.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 97, pp. 199–203. **Difficulty:** Expert.

**Primary topic:** Sequence analysis with ROW_NUMBER. **Also covers:** Dominant category per order, Conditional aggregation pivot, Window SUM over aggregates for row percentages, LEFT JOIN to keep customers without a second order.

**Business scenario.** The growth team sends a "come back" voucher a few days after a customer's first order. Today every voucher shows the same banner. The merchandising lead wants the banner to depend on what the customer bought first, but only if first-order category actually predicts the second order. If people who start with staples come back for staples, the banner should show staples; if they move to snacks, it should show snacks.

**Task.** For each customer, find the dominant category of their first order and of their second order. Count customers for every (first category, second category) pair, including customers who never placed a second order, and show each pair as a percentage of all customers who started in that first category.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
first_order_category : dominant category of the customer's first order
second_order_category : dominant category of the second order, or No second order
customers : customers on that path
pct_of_first_category : customers as a percentage of everyone with the same first_order_category , 2 dp
```

**Tables required:** Orders , OrderDetails , Products , Category.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** sequencing events per customer with ROW_NUMBER, collapsing a multi-category order to one label, pivoting two events onto one row, keeping customers with no second event, row percentages with a window over an aggregate.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.

### Interview follow-up (Step 9)

The team wants a lift-style comparison: for each first category, is the share of customers whose second order is in the same category higher than that category's share of all second orders? Write the query and explain what a value above 1 would mean for the banner.

### Sample input and expected output

*Illustrative example; each order has one category, so its dominant category is unambiguous.*

Input — `Category` and `Products`:

| CategoryID | CategoryName | ProductID |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Audio | 101 |
| 2 | Home | 102 |

Input — `Orders`:

| OrderID | CustomerID | OrderDate |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 10 | 2026-01-01 |
| 2 | 10 | 2026-01-10 |
| 3 | 20 | 2026-01-02 |

Input — `OrderDetails`:

| OrderDetailID | OrderID | ProductID | Quantity |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 101 | 2 |
| 2 | 2 | 102 | 1 |
| 3 | 3 | 101 | 1 |

Expected output:

| first_order_category | second_order_category | customers | pct_of_first_category |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Audio | Home | 1 | 50.00 |
| Audio | No second order | 1 | 50.00 |
