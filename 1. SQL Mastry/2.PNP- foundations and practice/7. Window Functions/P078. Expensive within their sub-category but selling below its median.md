# PNP Q078. Expensive within their sub-category but selling below its median

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 78, pp. 146–148. **Difficulty:** Hard.

**Primary topic:** PERCENT_RANK and PERCENTILE_CONT per group. **Also covers:** Multiple CTEs, Window vs ordered-set aggregates, LEFT JOIN to keep zero sellers, Minimum group size.

**Business scenario.** The pricing team suspects some premium products are priced above what customers will pay. Their test: a product sits in the top quarter of prices within its own sub-category, yet it has sold fewer units than the sub-category's median product. Those products are candidates for a price review. Comparing within the sub-category matters, because a 600-rupee face cream and a 600-rupee pressure cooker are not in the same price tier.

**Task.** For each product, compute its price percentile rank within its Sub_Category , its total units sold (0 if it never sold), and the median units of that sub-category, counting every product including zero sellers. Return products with a price percentile rank of at least 0.75 whose units are strictly below the sub-category median. Only judge sub-categories with at least 20 products. Sort by sub-category, then price rank (highest first), then ProductID .

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
Sub_Category
ProductID , Product , Sale_Price
price_pct_rank : PERCENT_RANK of Sale_Price within the sub-category, 2 decimals (0 = cheapest; exactly 1 only for a unique most
expensive product)
units_sold : total Quantity across all order lines, 0 for unsold products
subcat_median_units : median of units_sold across all products in the sub-category
```

**Tables required:** Products , OrderDetails.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** PERCENT_RANK() as a window function, PERCENTILE_CONT as an ordered-set aggregate (not usable with OVER in PostgreSQL), aggregating lines to the product grain before joining, keeping zero sellers, a minimum group size, joining two group-level results.
