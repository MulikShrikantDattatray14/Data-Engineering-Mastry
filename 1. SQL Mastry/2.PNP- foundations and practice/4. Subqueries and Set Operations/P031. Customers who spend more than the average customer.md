# PNP Q031. Customers who spend more than the average customer

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![MEDIUM](<../../_badges/medium.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 31, pp. 52–54. **Difficulty:** Medium.

**Primary topic:** Scalar subquery over a CTE. **Also covers:** Aggregation to customer grain, LEFT JOIN for names, Percentage difference.

**Business scenario.** The CRM team is building an invite list for an early-access programme. The rule is simple: invite customers whose lifetime spend is above what the average customer spends. Lifetime spend means the sum of Total_Order_Amount across all of a customer's orders. Refunds are not modelled, so this is gross spend.

**Task.** Return every customer whose total spend is greater than the average total spend per customer. Show the customer's spend, the average they were compared against, and how far above it they are in percent. Sort by spend, highest first.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
CustomerID
customer_name : first and last name, or Unknown customer when the ID has no Customers row
total_spend : sum of the customer's Total_Order_Amount
avg_customer_spend : average of total_spend across all ordering customers (2 dp)
pct_above_avg : how far total_spend is above that average, in percent (2 dp)
```

**Tables required:** Orders , Customers.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** aggregating to the customer grain in a CTE, scalar subquery that reads the CTE, comparing a row with a set-level value, percentage difference.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.

### Sample input and expected output

*Illustrative example; the source gives output columns but no sample rows. Only the columns needed for this example are shown.*

**Input — `Customers`**

| CustomerID | FirstName | LastName |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asha | Rao |
| 2 | Ben | Das |
| 3 | Cia | Sen |

**Input — `Orders`**

| OrderID | CustomerID | Total_Order_Amount |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1 | 300 |
| 11 | 2 | 100 |
| 12 | 3 | 200 |

**Expected output**

| CustomerID | customer_name | total_spend | avg_customer_spend | pct_above_avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asha Rao | 300 | 200.00 | 50.00 |

The customer spending average is (300 + 100 + 200) / 3 = 200.
