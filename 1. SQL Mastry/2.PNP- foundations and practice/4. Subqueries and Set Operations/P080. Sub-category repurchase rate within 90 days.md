# PNP Q080. Sub-category repurchase rate within 90 days

![NOT SOLVED YET](<../../_badges/not-solved-yet.svg>) ![HARD](<../../_badges/hard.svg>)

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 80, pp. 151–153. **Difficulty:** Hard.

**Primary topic:** Correlated EXISTS with a later-date condition. **Also covers:** Multiple CTEs, DISTINCT at the customer-sub-category-day grain, Observation window with anchored cut-off, HAVING minimum sample, Semi-join vs per-row subplan.

**Business scenario.** The merchandising team is choosing where to launch "subscribe and save". A sub-category is a good fit when people who buy from it come back for it soon: skin care or spices, rather than crockery. They define the repurchase rate as: of the customers who bought from the sub-category, the share who bought from it again on a later day within 90 days of their first purchase there. They work at sub-category level because a subscription is sold per need (tea, detergent, shampoo), not per SKU, and because most of the 22,000 products have too few buyers to judge on their own. Sub-categories with fewer than 30 eligible buyers are too small to judge.

**Task.** For each Sub_Category , count its eligible buyers and how many of them bought from it again on a later day within 90 days of their first purchase there. A buyer is eligible only if that first purchase is at least 90 days before the latest OrderDate in the data, so every buyer has had a full 90 days to come back. Return sub-categories with at least 30 eligible buyers, sorted by repurchase rate, highest first.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
Sub_Category
buyers : eligible customers who bought from the sub-category
repeat_buyers : of those, customers who bought from it again on a later day within 90 days
repurchase_rate_pct : repeat_buyers as a percentage of buyers , 2 decimals
```

**Tables required:** Orders , OrderDetails , Products.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** correlated EXISTS with a range condition on the outer row, de-duplicating to the grain of the question, an anchored observation window, minimum sample size with HAVING, and why the position of EXISTS decides whether PostgreSQL can turn it into a semi-join.

The source is a text-based PDF; no separate question image was supplied.

### Sample input and expected output

*Illustrative compact input: ranges represent individual customer purchases after joining Orders, OrderDetails, and Products. The latest OrderDate in the data is 2026-04-15, so all 30 buyers who first bought on 2026-01-01 have a full 90-day observation window.*

Input — purchase events:

| CustomerID | Sub_Category | OrderDate | Number of underlying rows |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 1–30 (one row per customer) | Tea | 2026-01-01 | 30 |
| 1–6 (one later row per customer) | Tea | 2026-01-20 | 6 |
| 99 | Other | 2026-04-15 | 1 |

Expected output:

| Sub_Category | buyers | repeat_buyers | repurchase_rate_pct |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tea | 30 | 6 | 20.00 |
