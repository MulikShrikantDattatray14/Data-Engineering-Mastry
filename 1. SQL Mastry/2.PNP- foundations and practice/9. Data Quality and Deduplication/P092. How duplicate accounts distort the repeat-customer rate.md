# PNP Q092. How duplicate accounts distort the repeat-customer rate

## Question and image

**Source:** [100 SQL Interview Questions](<../Source Materials/Updated_100_SQL_Interview_Questions.pdf>), question 92, pp. 184–186. **Difficulty:** Expert.

**Primary topic:** Deduplication impact on metrics. **Also covers:** Survivor mapping with a window MIN, Normalised match key, Metric recomputed before and after, UNION ALL, Conditional aggregation.

**Business scenario.** The growth team reports the repeat-customer rate to leadership every month. Support has noticed that some people sign up again with the same email in a different case, or with a stray space, because they forgot they had an account. Leadership wants to know whether the reported rate is wrong, by how much, and in which direction, before the data platform team spends a quarter building a proper customer merge.

**Task.** Compute the repeat-customer rate twice: once with each CustomerID treated as a customer, and once with accounts merged when their normalised email ( LOWER(TRIM(Email)) ) matches. A repeat customer is one with two or more orders. Only customers with at least one order count. Return one row per method, plus the total orders in each method as a control.

**Expected output (as supplied):**

```text
method : Raw CustomerID or Merged by email
customers : customers (or merged people) with at least one order
repeat_customers : those with two or more orders
repeat_rate_pct : repeat_customers / customers Ã— 100 , 2 dp
orders : total orders attributed, which must be identical in both rows
```

**Tables required:** Customers , Orders.

**SQL concepts named in the source:** building a match key, mapping every account to a survivor with a window MIN, LEFT JOIN so orders with unknown customers are not lost, computing one metric two ways with UNION ALL, control totals.
