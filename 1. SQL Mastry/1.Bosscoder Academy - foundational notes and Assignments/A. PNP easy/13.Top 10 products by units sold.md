# 13. Top 10 products by units sold

## Question and image

Using the Northwind database, the inventory team wants the ten best-selling products by total volume (not revenue). For each product, sum the `Quantity` sold across all order lines.

Tables involved: `"Order Details"`, `Products`.

Return exactly two columns:

- `ProductName`: the product name.
- `TotalUnitsSold`: the total quantity sold for that product.

Sort by `TotalUnitsSold` in **descending** order; break ties by `ProductName` in **ascending (A–Z)** order. Return only the **top 10 rows**.

**Expected output:** `ProductName`, `TotalUnitsSold` · 10 rows · order matters.

## My solution — SQLite

```sql
select ProductName , sum ("Order Details".Quantity) as TotalUnitsSold
from "Order Details"
join Products on Products.ProductID = "Order Details".ProductID
group by Products.ProductID
order by TotalUnitsSold desc , ProductName asc
limit 10
```

**1. Attach the product name to each order line**

The requested name is in Products, while the sold quantity is in Order Details. Join on ProductID and keep the quantity from each order line.

```sql
SELECT "Order Details".OrderID, Products.ProductID, ProductName, Quantity
FROM "Order Details"
JOIN Products ON Products.ProductID = "Order Details".ProductID
ORDER BY "Order Details".OrderID;
```

Result:

| OrderID | ProductID | ProductName | Quantity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 101 | 1 | Tea | 15 |
| 102 | 1 | Tea | 5 |
| 103 | 2 | Coffee | 18 |
| 104 | 3 | Bread | 16 |
| 105 | 4 | Milk | 14 |
| 106 | 5 | Rice | 12 |
| 107 | 6 | Beans | 10 |
| 108 | 7 | Juice | 8 |
| 109 | 8 | Pasta | 6 |
| 110 | 9 | Salt | 4 |
| 111 | 10 | Sugar | 2 |
| 112 | 11 | Water | 2 |

Tea has two order lines. Both must contribute to its eventual total; counting the lines would count purchases, not units.

**2. Total the units sold for each product**

GROUP BY ProductID combines a product's order lines. SUM(Quantity) measures total units rather than the number of orders.

```sql
SELECT Products.ProductID, ProductName, SUM("Order Details".Quantity) AS TotalUnitsSold
FROM "Order Details"
JOIN Products ON Products.ProductID = "Order Details".ProductID
GROUP BY Products.ProductID;
```

Result:

| ProductID | ProductName | TotalUnitsSold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tea | 20 |
| 2 | Coffee | 18 |
| 3 | Bread | 16 |
| 4 | Milk | 14 |
| 5 | Rice | 12 |
| 6 | Beans | 10 |
| 7 | Juice | 8 |
| 8 | Pasta | 6 |
| 9 | Salt | 4 |
| 10 | Sugar | 2 |
| 11 | Water | 2 |

Tea becomes 15 + 5 = 20 units. There are eleven product groups, so LIMIT 10 will remove one group after sorting.

**3. Rank all product totals before limiting**

Sort by TotalUnitsSold descending. When totals tie, sort the product names ascending so the cutoff is predictable.

```sql
SELECT ProductName, SUM("Order Details".Quantity) AS TotalUnitsSold
FROM "Order Details"
JOIN Products ON Products.ProductID = "Order Details".ProductID
GROUP BY Products.ProductID
ORDER BY TotalUnitsSold DESC, ProductName ASC;
```

Result:

| ProductName | TotalUnitsSold |
| --- | --- |
| Tea | 20 |
| Coffee | 18 |
| Bread | 16 |

| Rice | 12 |

| Pasta | 6 |

Sugar and Water both have two units. Sugar sorts before Water, so the final ten-row limit includes Sugar and excludes Water.

**4. Assemble the final query**

Combine the steps using the saved solution. The query below preserves the original logic.

```sql
select ProductName , sum ("Order Details".Quantity) as TotalUnitsSold
from "Order Details"
join Products on Products.ProductID = "Order Details".ProductID
group by Products.ProductID
order by TotalUnitsSold desc , ProductName asc
limit 10
```

## My solution — SQL Server

```sql
select TOP (10) ProductName , sum ([Order Details].Quantity) as TotalUnitsSold
from [Order Details]
join Products on Products.ProductID = [Order Details].ProductID
group by Products.ProductID, Products.ProductName
order by TotalUnitsSold desc , ProductName asc
```

## Official solution

```sql
SELECT p.ProductName,
       SUM(od.Quantity) AS TotalUnitsSold
FROM "Order Details" od
JOIN Products p ON od.ProductID = p.ProductID
GROUP BY p.ProductName
ORDER BY TotalUnitsSold DESC, p.ProductName ASC
LIMIT 10;
```

### What this tests

Joining tables, summing quantities, ranking results through sorting, and limiting output.

### The approach

Join order details to products; sum Quantity per product name; sort total units descending and name ascending; keep ten rows.

### Why this way

The join supplies product names and GROUP BY gathers each product's sales quantities for SUM.

### Common mistakes

Missing or incorrect joins, omitting grouping, or filtering out valid order lines before calculating total units.
