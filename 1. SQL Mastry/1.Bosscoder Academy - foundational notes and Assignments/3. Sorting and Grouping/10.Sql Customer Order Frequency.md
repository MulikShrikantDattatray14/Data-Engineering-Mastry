## My solution — SQLite

```sql
SELECT
    C.customer_id AS customer_id,
    C.name AS name
FROM Customers AS C
JOIN Orders AS O
    ON C.customer_id = O.customer_id
JOIN Product AS P
    ON O.product_id = P.product_id
WHERE O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
  AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
GROUP BY C.customer_id, C.name
HAVING SUM(
    CASE
        WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
         AND O.order_date < '2020-07-01'
        THEN P.price * O.quantity
        ELSE 0
    END
) >= 100
AND SUM(
    CASE
        WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-07-01'
         AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
        THEN P.price * O.quantity
        ELSE 0
    END
) >= 100;
```

### Key learnings

- An order's spending amount is `P.price * O.quantity`. Summing only `price` would ignore the number of units purchased.
- The outer `WHERE` limits input rows to June and July 2020 before grouping.
- Conditional aggregation uses `SUM(CASE WHEN ... THEN amount ELSE 0 END)` to calculate separate totals within the same customer group.
- June and July require separate conditional sums connected with `AND` because the customer must spend at least $100 in each month.
- A combined two-month total would be incorrect because a customer could spend heavily in one month and less than $100 in the other.
- `HAVING` filters customers after their monthly aggregate totals have been calculated.
- Half-open date ranges such as `>= '2020-06-01' AND < '2020-07-01'` include the entire month without depending on the number of days in it.
- Invisible non-breaking spaces copied from formatted text can be interpreted as part of an identifier. Plain SQL text avoids errors such as `no such column`.

### Step-by-step explanation with one example

We need one row per customer who spent at least 100 in June 2020 AND at least 100 in July 2020. Use the same Customers, Product, and Orders example throughout. The two months need separate totals; a large combined total is not enough.

**Starting data**

`Customers`:

| customer_id | name | country |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Winston | USA |
| 2 | Jonathan | Peru |
| 3 | Moustafa | Egypt |

`Product`:

| product_id | description | price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | LC Phone | 300 |
| 20 | LC T-Shirt | 10 |
| 30 | LC Book | 45 |
| 40 | LC Keychain | 2 |

`Orders`:

| order_id | customer_id | product_id | order_date | quantity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | 10 | 2020-06-10 | 1 |
| 2 | 1 | 20 | 2020-07-01 | 1 |
| 3 | 1 | 30 | 2020-07-08 | 2 |
| 4 | 2 | 10 | 2020-06-15 | 2 |
| 5 | 2 | 40 | 2020-07-01 | 10 |
| 6 | 3 | 20 | 2020-06-24 | 2 |
| 7 | 3 | 30 | 2020-06-25 | 2 |
| 9 | 3 | 30 | 2020-05-08 | 3 |

**1. Join names, quantities, and unit prices**

Orders links each customer to a product and quantity. Join Customers for the name and Product for the unit price; multiply price by quantity for the spending on that order.

```sql
SELECT O.order_id, C.customer_id, C.name, O.order_date,
       P.price, O.quantity, P.price * O.quantity AS order_spend
FROM Customers AS C
JOIN Orders AS O ON C.customer_id = O.customer_id
JOIN Product AS P ON O.product_id = P.product_id
ORDER BY O.order_id;
```

Result:

| order_id | customer_id | name | order_date | price | quantity | order_spend |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | Winston | 2020-06-10 | 300 | 1 | 300 |
| 2 | 1 | Winston | 2020-07-01 | 10 | 1 | 10 |
| 3 | 1 | Winston | 2020-07-08 | 45 | 2 | 90 |
| 4 | 2 | Jonathan | 2020-06-15 | 300 | 2 | 600 |
| 5 | 2 | Jonathan | 2020-07-01 | 2 | 10 | 20 |
| 6 | 3 | Moustafa | 2020-06-24 | 10 | 2 | 20 |
| 7 | 3 | Moustafa | 2020-06-25 | 45 | 2 | 90 |
| 9 | 3 | Moustafa | 2020-05-08 | 45 | 3 | 135 |

Order 4 costs 300 * 2 = 600. Order 9 costs 45 * 3 = 135, but it occurred in May and must not contribute to either required month.

**2. Keep only June and July orders**

Filter by dates before grouping, so irrelevant months cannot enter the customer totals. The upper bound August 1 is excluded.

```sql
SELECT O.order_id, C.name, O.order_date,
       P.price * O.quantity AS order_spend
FROM Customers AS C
JOIN Orders AS O ON C.customer_id = O.customer_id
JOIN Product AS P ON O.product_id = P.product_id
WHERE O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
  AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
ORDER BY O.order_id;
```

Result:

| order_id | name | order_date | order_spend |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Winston | 2020-06-10 | 300 |
| 2 | Winston | 2020-07-01 | 10 |
| 3 | Winston | 2020-07-08 | 90 |
| 4 | Jonathan | 2020-06-15 | 600 |
| 5 | Jonathan | 2020-07-01 | 20 |
| 6 | Moustafa | 2020-06-24 | 20 |
| 7 | Moustafa | 2020-06-25 | 90 |

Order 9 is removed. Winston has one June order and two July orders; Jonathan has one in each month; Moustafa has two June orders and no July order.

**3. Assign every remaining order to its month**

Each CASE contributes the order amount to the correct month and zero to the other month. This lets both totals be calculated inside the same customer group.

```sql
SELECT O.order_id, C.name,
       CASE WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
          AND O.order_date < '2020-07-01'
     THEN P.price * O.quantity ELSE 0 END AS june_amount,
       CASE WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-07-01'
          AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
     THEN P.price * O.quantity ELSE 0 END AS july_amount
FROM Customers AS C
JOIN Orders AS O ON C.customer_id = O.customer_id
JOIN Product AS P ON O.product_id = P.product_id
WHERE O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
  AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
ORDER BY O.order_id;
```

Result:

| order_id | name | june_amount | july_amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Winston | 300 | 0 |
| 2 | Winston | 0 | 10 |
| 3 | Winston | 0 | 90 |
| 4 | Jonathan | 600 | 0 |
| 5 | Jonathan | 0 | 20 |
| 6 | Moustafa | 20 | 0 |
| 7 | Moustafa | 90 | 0 |

Winston's July orders contribute 10 and 90 to July, while his June order contributes 300 to June. Zero means that row makes no contribution to the other month.

**4. Add monthly contributions per customer**

GROUP BY customer_id, name creates one result group per customer. SUM over the CASE expressions produces separate June and July totals.

```sql
SELECT C.customer_id, C.name,
       SUM(CASE WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
          AND O.order_date < '2020-07-01'
     THEN P.price * O.quantity ELSE 0 END) AS june_total,
       SUM(CASE WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-07-01'
          AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
     THEN P.price * O.quantity ELSE 0 END) AS july_total
FROM Customers AS C
JOIN Orders AS O ON C.customer_id = O.customer_id
JOIN Product AS P ON O.product_id = P.product_id
WHERE O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
  AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
GROUP BY C.customer_id, C.name
ORDER BY C.customer_id;
```

Result:

| customer_id | name | june_total | july_total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Winston | 300 | 100 |
| 2 | Jonathan | 600 | 20 |
| 3 | Moustafa | 110 | 0 |

Winston has 300 and 100; Jonathan has 600 and 20; Moustafa has 110 and 0. HAVING must require both totals to be >= 100.

**5. Assemble the final query**

Apply both aggregate thresholds with AND, then select the requested customer ID and name. This preserves the saved conditional aggregation approach.

```sql
SELECT
    C.customer_id AS customer_id,
    C.name AS name
FROM Customers AS C
JOIN Orders AS O
    ON C.customer_id = O.customer_id
JOIN Product AS P
    ON O.product_id = P.product_id
WHERE O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
  AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
GROUP BY C.customer_id, C.name
HAVING SUM(
    CASE
        WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
         AND O.order_date < '2020-07-01'
        THEN P.price * O.quantity
        ELSE 0
    END
) >= 100
AND SUM(
    CASE
        WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-07-01'
         AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
        THEN P.price * O.quantity
        ELSE 0
    END
) >= 100;
```

Final result for this example:

| customer_id | name |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Winston |

**Check the result:** Manual check: Winston qualifies because 300 >= 100 and 10 + 90 = 100. Jonathan fails July despite spending 600 in June. Moustafa's June 20 + 90 = 110 is enough, but July is zero, so he fails. His May spending is irrelevant.

```sql
SELECT
    C.customer_id AS customer_id,

FROM Customers AS C
JOIN Orders AS O
    ON C.customer_id = O.customer_id
JOIN Product AS P
    ON O.product_id = P.product_id
WHERE O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
  AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
GROUP BY C.customer_id, C.name
HAVING SUM(
    CASE
        WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-06-01'
         AND O.order_date < '2020-07-01'
        THEN P.price * O.quantity
        ELSE 0
    END
) >= 100
AND SUM(
    CASE
        WHEN O.order_date >= '2020-07-01'
         AND O.order_date < '2020-08-01'
        THEN P.price * O.quantity
        ELSE 0
    END
) >= 100;
```

The same query works in SQLite and SQL Server.
> **Shared SQL processing note:** `FROM/JOIN → WHERE → GROUP BY → HAVING → SELECT → DISTINCT → ORDER BY → row limiting`. SQLite uses `LIMIT/OFFSET`; SQL Server uses `TOP` or `OFFSET/FETCH`. `WHERE` filters rows before grouping, while `HAVING` filters groups after aggregation. See [SQL Logical Query Processing Order](../0.%20SQL%20basics/SQL%20Logical%20Query%20Processing%20Order.md).
