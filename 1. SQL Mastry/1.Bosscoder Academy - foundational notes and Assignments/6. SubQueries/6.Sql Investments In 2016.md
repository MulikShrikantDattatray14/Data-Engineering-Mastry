# 6. SQL Investments In 2016

## Question and image

**6. Sql Investments In 2016**

Table: Insurance

| Column Name | Type |
|---|---|
| pid | int |
| tiv_2015 | float |
| tiv_2016 | float |
| lat | float |
| lon | float |

pid is the primary key (column with unique values) for this table.

Each row of this table contains information about one policy where:

pid is the policyholder's policy ID.

tiv_2015 is the total investment value in 2015 and tiv_2016 is the total investment value in 2016.

lat is the latitude of the policy holder's city. It's guaranteed that lat is not NULL.

lon is the longitude of the policy holder's city. It's guaranteed that lon is not NULL.

Write a solution to report the sum of all total investment values in 2016 tiv_2016, for all policyholders who:

1. have the same tiv_2015 value as one or more other policyholders, and
2. are not located in the same city as any other policyholder (i.e., the (lat, lon) attribute pairs must be unique).

**Example 1:**

Example:

Input:

Insurance table:

| pid | tiv_2015 | tiv_2016 | lat | lon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 5 | 10 | 10 |
| 2 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| 3 | 10 | 30 | 20 | 20 |
| 4 | 10 | 40 | 40 | 40 |

Output:

| tiv_2016 |
|---|
| 45.00 |

The first record in the table, like the last record, meets both of the two criteria.

The tiv_2015 value 10 is the same as the third and fourth records, and its location is unique.

The second record does not meet any of the two criteria. Its tiv_2015 is not like any other policyholders and its location is the same as the third record, which makes the third record fail, too.

So, the result is the sum of tiv_2016 of the first and last record, which is 45.

## My solution — SQLite

### Final solution

```sql
SELECT ROUND(SUM(tiv_2016), 2) AS tiv_2016
FROM Insurance
WHERE tiv_2015 IN (
    SELECT tiv_2015
    FROM Insurance
    GROUP BY tiv_2015
    HAVING COUNT(pid) >= 2
)
AND (lat, lon) IN (
    SELECT lat, lon
    FROM Insurance
    GROUP BY lat, lon
    HAVING COUNT(pid) = 1
);
```

### Step-by-step explanation with one example

The original `Insurance` table is used throughout, showing what each query produces.

1. **Start with the original data.**

   ```sql
   SELECT *
   FROM Insurance;
   ```

   Result:

   | pid | tiv_2015 | tiv_2016 | lat | lon |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | 1 | 10 | 5 | 10 | 10 |
   | 2 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 20 |
   | 3 | 10 | 30 | 20 | 20 |
   | 4 | 10 | 40 | 40 | 40 |

   We must identify the qualifying rows **before adding their `tiv_2016` values**.

2. **Count how many policies share each `tiv_2015` value.**

   ```sql
   SELECT
       tiv_2015,
       COUNT(pid) AS policy_count
   FROM Insurance
   GROUP BY tiv_2015;
   ```

   Result:

   | tiv_2015 | policy_count |
   |---:|---:|
   | 10 | 3 |
   | 20 | 1 |

   Value `10` belongs to PIDs **1, 3, and 4**. Value `20` belongs only to PID **2**.

3. **Keep only `tiv_2015` values appearing at least twice.**

   ```sql
   SELECT tiv_2015
   FROM Insurance
   GROUP BY tiv_2015
   HAVING COUNT(pid) >= 2;
   ```

   Result:

   | tiv_2015 |
   |---:|
   | 10 |

   `HAVING` removes the group for `20` because its count is only `1`.

   This gives us the **first eligibility list**. Policies **1, 3, and 4** have an investment value in this list.

4. **Count policies in each city.**

   ```sql
   SELECT
       lat,
       lon,
       COUNT(pid) AS policy_count
   FROM Insurance
   GROUP BY lat, lon;
   ```

   Result:

   | lat | lon | policy_count |
   |---:|---:|---:|
   | 10 | 10 | 1 |
   | 20 | 20 | 2 |
   | 40 | 40 | 1 |

   PIDs **2 and 3** belong to the same city, `(20, 20)`. They are counted together because we group by **both coordinates**, without `pid`.

5. **Keep only cities containing exactly one policy.**

   ```sql
   SELECT lat, lon
   FROM Insurance
   GROUP BY lat, lon
   HAVING COUNT(pid) = 1;
   ```

   Result:

   | lat | lon |
   |---:|---:|
   | 10 | 10 |
   | 40 | 40 |

   The city `(20, 20)` is removed because it contains two policies.

   This gives us the **second eligibility list**. Policies **1 and 4** have cities in this list.

6. **Apply both eligibility lists to the original rows.**

   ```sql
   SELECT *
   FROM Insurance
   WHERE tiv_2015 IN (
       SELECT tiv_2015
       FROM Insurance
       GROUP BY tiv_2015
       HAVING COUNT(pid) >= 2
   )
   AND (lat, lon) IN (
       SELECT lat, lon
       FROM Insurance
       GROUP BY lat, lon
       HAVING COUNT(pid) = 1
   );
   ```

   Result:

   | pid | tiv_2015 | tiv_2016 | lat | lon |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | 1 | 10 | 5 | 10 | 10 |
   | 4 | 10 | 40 | 40 | 40 |

   Here is how the filtering works:

   | pid | Passes investment check? | Passes city check? | Final decision |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | Yes | Yes | Keep |
   | 2 | No | No | Exclude |
   | 3 | Yes | No | Exclude |
   | 4 | Yes | Yes | Keep |

   **Both subqueries read the entire original table.** We do not remove PID 2 first and then mistakenly treat PID 3's city as unique.

7. **Replace `SELECT *` with the final calculation.**

   The remaining `tiv_2016` values are **5 and 40**, so we add them.

   Complete query:

   ```sql
   SELECT ROUND(SUM(tiv_2016), 2) AS tiv_2016
   FROM Insurance
   WHERE tiv_2015 IN (
       SELECT tiv_2015
       FROM Insurance
       GROUP BY tiv_2015
       HAVING COUNT(pid) >= 2
   )
   AND (lat, lon) IN (
       SELECT lat, lon
       FROM Insurance
       GROUP BY lat, lon
       HAVING COUNT(pid) = 1
   );
   ```

   Calculation:

   ```text
   SUM(tiv_2016) = 5 + 40 = 45
   ```

   Result, shown to two decimal places:

   | tiv_2016 |
   |---:|
   | 45.00 |

   `SUM` combines the qualifying rows into one total. `ROUND(..., 2)` rounds that total to two decimal places.

## My solution — SQL Server

```sql
SELECT ROUND(SUM(i.tiv_2016), 2) AS tiv_2016
FROM Insurance AS i
WHERE i.tiv_2015 IN (
    SELECT tiv_2015
    FROM Insurance
    GROUP BY tiv_2015
    HAVING COUNT(pid) >= 2
)
AND EXISTS (
    SELECT 1
    FROM Insurance AS city
    WHERE city.lat = i.lat
      AND city.lon = i.lon
    GROUP BY city.lat, city.lon
    HAVING COUNT(city.pid) = 1
);
```
