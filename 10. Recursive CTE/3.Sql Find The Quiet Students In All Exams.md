# 3. SQL Find The Quiet Students In All Exams

## Question and image

**Assignment question 1.** Saved as Question 3 in this section because Questions 1 and 2 are classroom practice problems.

A quiet student took at least one exam and did not score the highest or the lowest score in any exam they took.

Write a solution to report the quiet students as (student_id, student_name). Do not return a student who never took an exam. Return the result ordered by student_id.

**Example 1**

Student:

| student_id | student_name |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Daniel |
| 2 | Jade |
| 3 | Stella |
| 4 | Jonathan |
| 5 | Will |

Exam:

| exam_id | student_id | score |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 1 | 70 |
| 10 | 2 | 80 |
| 10 | 3 | 90 |
| 20 | 1 | 80 |
| 30 | 1 | 70 |
| 30 | 3 | 80 |
| 30 | 4 | 90 |
| 40 | 1 | 60 |
| 40 | 2 | 70 |
| 40 | 4 | 80 |

Output:

| student_id | student_name |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Jade |

For exam 10, Daniel and Stella have the lowest and highest scores. Daniel has both the lowest and highest score in exam 20. Daniel and Jonathan have the lowest and highest scores in exams 30 and 40. Jade never has an extreme score. Will took no exam, so only Jade is returned.

## My solution — SQLite

```sql
WITH exam_scores AS (
    SELECT
        E.exam_id,
        E.student_id,
        S.student_name,
        E.score,
        MIN(E.score) OVER (PARTITION BY E.exam_id) AS lowest_score,
        MAX(E.score) OVER (PARTITION BY E.exam_id) AS highest_score
    FROM Exam AS E
    INNER JOIN Student AS S
        ON E.student_id = S.student_id
),
student_flags AS (
    SELECT
        student_id,
        student_name,
        MAX(
            CASE
                WHEN score = lowest_score
                  OR score = highest_score THEN 1
                ELSE 0
            END
        ) AS had_extreme_score
    FROM exam_scores
    GROUP BY student_id, student_name
)
SELECT student_id, student_name
FROM student_flags
WHERE had_extreme_score = 0
ORDER BY student_id;
```

### Step-by-step explanation with one example

**1. Join exam attempts to students.** One Exam row is one student's attempt at one exam. The INNER JOIN adds the student's name to each attempt:

```sql
SELECT E.exam_id, E.student_id, S.student_name, E.score
FROM Exam AS E
INNER JOIN Student AS S
    ON E.student_id = S.student_id
ORDER BY E.exam_id, E.student_id;
```

| exam_id | student_id | student_name | score |
|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 10 | 1 | Daniel | 70 |
| 10 | 2 | Jade | 80 |
| 10 | 3 | Stella | 90 |
| 20 | 1 | Daniel | 80 |
| 30 | 1 | Daniel | 70 |
| 30 | 3 | Stella | 80 |
| 30 | 4 | Jonathan | 90 |
| 40 | 1 | Daniel | 60 |
| 40 | 2 | Jade | 70 |
| 40 | 4 | Jonathan | 80 |

Will (student 5) has no Exam row, so the INNER JOIN excludes him. This satisfies the requirement to omit students who never took an exam.

**2. Attach the lowest and highest score for each exam.** Both window functions partition by exam_id only, so every participant in the same exam sees the same two extremes. They keep all ten attempt rows. An ORDER BY inside these windows is unnecessary because we need each exam's overall minimum and maximum.

```sql
MIN(E.score) OVER (PARTITION BY E.exam_id) AS lowest_score,
MAX(E.score) OVER (PARTITION BY E.exam_id) AS highest_score
```

| exam_id | student_name | score | lowest_score | highest_score |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | Daniel | 70 | 70 | 90 |
| 10 | Jade | 80 | 70 | 90 |
| 10 | Stella | 90 | 70 | 90 |
| 20 | Daniel | 80 | 80 | 80 |
| 30 | Daniel | 70 | 70 | 90 |
| 30 | Stella | 80 | 70 | 90 |
| 30 | Jonathan | 90 | 70 | 90 |
| 40 | Daniel | 60 | 60 | 80 |
| 40 | Jade | 70 | 60 | 80 |
| 40 | Jonathan | 80 | 60 | 80 |

Exam 20 has just Daniel, so its lowest and highest are both 80. A score tied for either extreme also disqualifies that student.

**3. Mark extreme attempts.** Inside student_flags, the CASE returns 1 when an attempt equals that exam's minimum or maximum, otherwise 0.

```sql
CASE
    WHEN score = lowest_score OR score = highest_score THEN 1
    ELSE 0
END
```

| student_id | student_name | exam_id | score | extreme flag |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Daniel | 10 | 70 | 1 |
| 2 | Jade | 10 | 80 | 0 |
| 3 | Stella | 10 | 90 | 1 |
| 1 | Daniel | 20 | 80 | 1 |
| 1 | Daniel | 30 | 70 | 1 |
| 3 | Stella | 30 | 80 | 0 |
| 4 | Jonathan | 30 | 90 | 1 |
| 1 | Daniel | 40 | 60 | 1 |
| 2 | Jade | 40 | 70 | 0 |
| 4 | Jonathan | 40 | 80 | 1 |

**4. Make one decision per student.** Group by student_id and student_name. MAX of the 0/1 flags is 1 if the student had an extreme score in **any** exam. This is why we cannot simply remove extreme rows: Stella's exam 30 row has a 0, but her exam 10 row has a 1, so she must be excluded entirely.

| student_id | student_name | flags across exams | had_extreme_score |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Daniel | 1, 1, 1, 1 | 1 |
| 2 | Jade | 0, 0 | 0 |
| 3 | Stella | 1, 0 | 1 |
| 4 | Jonathan | 1, 1 | 1 |

**5. Keep quiet students.** `WHERE had_extreme_score = 0` retains students who took at least one exam but never scored an extreme. `ORDER BY student_id` gives the required order:

| student_id | student_name |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Jade |

### Alternative solution using a recursive CTE

This version examines each student's exams in exam_id order. It carries a 0/1 flag meaning "this student has scored an extreme in an exam seen so far."

```sql
WITH RECURSIVE exam_limits AS (
    SELECT
        E.exam_id,
        E.student_id,
        S.student_name,
        E.score,
        MIN(E.score) OVER (PARTITION BY E.exam_id) AS lowest_score,
        MAX(E.score) OVER (PARTITION BY E.exam_id) AS highest_score
    FROM Exam AS E
    INNER JOIN Student AS S
        ON E.student_id = S.student_id
),
numbered_attempts AS (
    SELECT
        exam_id,
        student_id,
        student_name,
        ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
            PARTITION BY student_id
            ORDER BY exam_id
        ) AS rn,
        COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY student_id) AS exam_count,
        CASE
            WHEN score = lowest_score OR score = highest_score THEN 1
            ELSE 0
        END AS extreme_flag
    FROM exam_limits
),
exam_walk AS (
    SELECT
        student_id,
        student_name,
        rn,
        exam_count,
        extreme_flag AS had_extreme_score
    FROM numbered_attempts
    WHERE rn = 1

    UNION ALL

    SELECT
        A.student_id,
        A.student_name,
        A.rn,
        A.exam_count,
        CASE
            WHEN W.had_extreme_score = 1 OR A.extreme_flag = 1 THEN 1
            ELSE 0
        END AS had_extreme_score
    FROM exam_walk AS W
    INNER JOIN numbered_attempts AS A
        ON A.student_id = W.student_id
       AND A.rn = W.rn + 1
)
SELECT student_id, student_name
FROM exam_walk
WHERE rn = exam_count
  AND had_extreme_score = 0
ORDER BY student_id;
```

**1. Prepare one row per exam attempt.** `exam_limits` attaches each exam's lowest and highest score to every participant. `numbered_attempts` then orders each student's exams, counts how many they took, and flags an extreme score.

| student | exam_id | rn | exam_count | extreme_flag |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Daniel | 10 | 1 | 4 | 1 |
| Daniel | 20 | 2 | 4 | 1 |
| Daniel | 30 | 3 | 4 | 1 |
| Daniel | 40 | 4 | 4 | 1 |
| Jade | 10 | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| Jade | 40 | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Stella | 10 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| Stella | 30 | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Jonathan | 30 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| Jonathan | 40 | 2 | 2 | 1 |

Will has no exam, so the INNER JOIN in `exam_limits` keeps him out. If an exam has one participant, that score is both its minimum and maximum, producing extreme_flag = 1.

**2. Start and advance the walk.** The anchor of `exam_walk` starts at rn = 1 for each student. The recursive member joins to that same student's next attempt (`A.rn = W.rn + 1`) and carries 1 forward once any attempt is extreme. The recursion stops naturally when no next attempt exists.

For Jade and Stella, the walk shows why one quiet exam cannot cancel an earlier extreme:

| student | rn | current extreme_flag | had_extreme_score after this exam |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jade | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Jade | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| Stella | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Stella | 2 | 0 | 1 |

**3. Keep only each student's final state.** `rn = exam_count` identifies the last recursive row for that student:

| student | rn | exam_count | had_extreme_score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Daniel | 4 | 4 | 1 |
| Jade | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Stella | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| Jonathan | 2 | 2 | 1 |

The final `had_extreme_score = 0` filter leaves Jade. The recursive walk is bounded by each student's number of exam attempts.

### Recursive CTE alternative — SQL Server

Use `;WITH` instead of `WITH RECURSIVE`. A student can take more than 100 exams, so `OPTION (MAXRECURSION 0)` avoids SQL Server's default recursion limit; the increasing row number still makes the walk finite.

        MAX(E.score) OVER (PARTITION BY E.exam_id) AS highest_score

        CASE
            WHEN score = lowest_score OR score = highest_score THEN 1

exam_walk AS (
    SELECT
        student_id,
        student_name,

        exam_count,
        extreme_flag AS had_extreme_score

    WHERE rn = 1
